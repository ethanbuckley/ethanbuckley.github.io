Research

Two studies of language development

Both at Down Syndrome Education International, both preliminary. One pools vocabulary data from fourteen studies. The other re-analyses a randomised reading-and-language trial.

Study 01 · Vocabulary growth

Vocabulary development in children with Down syndrome

A pooled Bayesian study of the words children understand, say and sign. It draws on parent-report checklists from fourteen datasets in six countries, put onto a common scale.

VG14 test-tier fit, 4 September 2026  ·  14 datasets  ·  1,424 assessments  ·  reported to age 6  ·  typically-developing comparisons from the July 2026 fits  ·  Preliminary findings

My contribution

I built the signing and within-child longitudinal models, the DuckDB pipeline that puts the fourteen datasets on one scale, and the descriptive and comprehension-gap reports. I also wrote the report’s data chapter: what each source collected, and how checklists of different lengths are scored against the 810-word inventory. Data validation has since expanded the pool to 777 children; the figures here still show the September fit.

Vocabulary findings

Comprehension precedes spoken vocabulary

The production ratio is the share of understood words a child also says. It increases with age, reaching about 44% by age four and 64% by six, the oldest age the model reports.

Greater delay in spoken vocabulary

By around age two, most children with Down syndrome say fewer words than the lowest-scoring tenth of typically-developing children. Understanding is delayed too, but by less.

The comprehension–production gap narrows with vocabulary size

At the same level of comprehension, children with Down syndrome say a smaller share of the words they know. The difference peaks at around 13 percentage points at 50–150 understood words, then fades as vocabulary grows.

Signing contributes to early expressive vocabulary

Including signed words reduces the estimated gap in expressive vocabulary, particularly between ages two and three. The delay in spoken vocabulary remains substantial.

Study 02 · Reading & language

Effects of a reading-and-language intervention

A re-analysis of the RLI trial (Burgoyne et al., 2012). 54 children with Down syndrome were randomly assigned to daily one-to-one reading-and-language teaching, or to a waiting list. A causal diagram distinguishes randomised intervention effects from associations that may be confounded by general ability.

RLI randomised trial  ·  54 children  ·  4 assessment waves  ·  only the randomised effects are causal  ·  Preliminary findings

My contribution

I developed intention-to-treat, difference-in-differences, mediation and mechanism models within the study’s causal framework, alongside gradient-boosting models for predictor ranking and the results reports. This work comprised 124 merged pull requests between June and August 2026.

Reading-trial findings and causal design

Evidence of improved decoding skills

The intervention improved letter-sound knowledge, blending, word reading and the expressive vocabulary explicitly taught.

No clear improvement in broader vocabulary or grammar

Benefits were concentrated in directly taught skills, with limited evidence of transfer to broader language abilities. This is consistent with the original trial’s conclusions.

Non-randomised results are associations

Baseline ability predicts later outcomes, but associations with subsequent progress remain confounded by general ability. These results are reported as associations rather than causal effects.

Interpretation and limitations

Both studies are exploratory and the findings are preliminary. Estimates are summarised by posterior distributions and credible intervals. Apart from the randomised teaching effects in the RLI trial, these results are associations, not proven causes.

DSE production system · images

Teaching-image pipeline

I built the pipeline that generated 7,351 photographic teaching images for an early-vocabulary programme, from written specifications for single words, phrases, sentences and named characters who recur across the set. Batch inference halved the unit cost, and a rerun skips anything whose request hash it has already produced. When one image is wrong I fix its glossary entry. When a kind of error recurs I add a prompt rule for the whole programme.

Implementation detail

I chose background colours that separate each subject from its surroundings without changing its natural colour, because reduced contrast sensitivity is common in Down syndrome.

First production set delivered July 2026 · manually reviewed · no validated image-quality claims

DSE production system · audio

Spoken-word audio pipeline

I produced recordings of 3,407 curriculum words, phrases and sentences, each read by a male and a female voice in four English accents. I chose the voices by blind A/B listening across four commercial providers, ranked them with a Bradley–Terry model and bootstrap intervals, then calibrated each to one measured pace, because the same speed setting gives different voices different paces.

Implementation detail

The scorer reports 90% intervals and flags voices that cannot be separated from the top-ranked one. I found and fixed a tie-handling error that had been inventing ranking differences.

Sixteen voices selected across eight accents · four accents delivered · frequent ties leave uncertainty in intermediate rankings

  • Python
  • CLI
  • blind A/B arena
  • Bradley–Terry
  • bootstrap intervals

DSE production system · video

Video-generation workflow

I set up the workflow for short clips that illustrate everyday verbs. Versioned prompt specifications generate the production instructions, and a validation step stops a clip being catalogued against an outdated brief. After the API trials failed to produce usable clips, generation runs through the provider’s web interface.

Implementation detail

An audit I ran found that four of the eight validated prompts had drifted from their approved specifications. Generating the instructions directly from the specifications, with an automated consistency check, closed that gap.

Specifications drafted for approximately sixty verbs; eight validated · generation tools recorded in asset provenance

DSE production system · catalogue

Asset audit and catalogue integration

I wrote the pipeline that audits, repairs, uploads and reconciles 23,846 draft assets. Audio review combines measurements with listening checks. Each approved repair is recorded, so a later run keeps the corrected file rather than regenerating it.

Implementation detail

Uploads work from an approved inventory, and a content hash links each file to its catalogue record. The curriculum audio in four accents sits in the catalogue as drafts until a person approves it for publication.

Connection timeouts of 20–30 seconds · expired sessions stop the run · reconciliation verifies catalogue records against file hashes

Independent project

S&P 500 stock screener

A two-stage screener for the S&P 500. An XGBoost classifier, trained on ~1.3 million daily observations, ranks long and short candidates, and FinBERT analyses news sentiment for the highest-ranked candidates. Training labels use the triple-barrier method. In walk-forward testing from 2020 to 2026 the top-15 selections had an approximately 48% success rate against a 29% base rate. A Streamlit dashboard runs it live, with a Monte Carlo value-at-risk simulator.

Expanding-window validation · limitations reported: survivorship bias, no transaction costs

Approach

Methods and tools

I work with Bayesian inference, machine learning and scientific computing. Every fit is checked with diagnostics and validation, and every result can be rebuilt from its pipeline.

Bayesian hierarchical modelling
Multilevel models with interpretable priors; study- and child-level random effects.
Gaussian processes
Hilbert-space (HSGP) smooths for age trajectories, which keep the fits tractable.
PyMC and NUTS sampling
Full-posterior MCMC via nutpie, gated on R̂, effective sample size and divergences.
Model checking and comparison
Prior and posterior predictive checks; PSIS-LOO and leave-one-study-out cross-validation.
Causal inference and DAGs
Randomised effects identified by design; confounded paths reported as association.
Gradient boosting and NLP
XGBoost and LightGBM with grouped cross-validation and SHAP; FinBERT for financial-news sentiment.
Generative-model evaluation
Blind A/B comparison with Bradley–Terry ranking and bootstrap intervals; prompts written as a specification.
Scientific computing and simulation
Vectorised simulations, like a spatial epidemic cellular automaton, validated against their analytical limits.
Data engineering
Fourteen datasets harmonised in a DuckDB view, with validation and ceiling guards.
Reproducible pipelines
Raw data to posteriors to figures to report, reproducible at three sampling tiers and tested in CI.

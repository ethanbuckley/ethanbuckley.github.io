Down Syndrome Education International · Reading & Language (RLI) Study

The intervention lifted reading, not broad vocabulary

The randomised effect of the teaching on each skill.

Each bar is the intervention’s effect on one skill, with its 95% credible interval. Children were randomised, so these read as cause, not correlation.

The gains cluster on the skills taught most directly: letter-sounds, blending and word reading. Broad, untaught vocabulary barely moved.

Learning transfers near what’s taught, not far. The effect fades with distance from instruction. This is the transfer gradient.

A bar clear of the dashed zero line is a credibly non-zero effect.
Intention-to-treat effects (log-odds) from the randomised phase of the RLI trial · Down syndrome, n = 54 · single-outcome Bayesian models · preliminary, integrated report in progress
